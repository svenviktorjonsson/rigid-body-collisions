Adaptive engine benchmark and calibration protocol

Prepared 2026-10-04. Status: pinned public source inputs and geometry downloaded;
no arbitrary-body reference solver or production-engine adapter has been run.
See benchmark-catalog.json for cases, exact source links, parameter provenance,
limitations, and suggested train/validation roles. Source manifests contain file
hashes and upstream commits. Upstream inputs and meshes remain unmodified.

1. Define a physical reference for each regime

Choose the same constitutive law, contact law, geometry, initial conditions,
boundary conditions and units for the fast and detailed representations wherever
possible. Use a nonlinear deformable continuum reference for appreciable strain;
use beam/plate models only where their assumptions apply. A tightly converged
rigid contact solve cannot validate deformation that its model excludes. IPC is
a candidate deformable reference, not an automatically exact physical oracle.
Its shipped paper-example timesteps and meshes need convergence checks.

Benchmark source parameters must retain provenance labels. A density, modulus
or friction value in an example file is not automatically a measurement. Record
material identity, specimen geometry, temperature, loading rate, uncertainty,
and the actual surface pair where experimental information is available. Do not
infer a rolling coefficient or tangential restitution from Young's modulus.

2. Establish reference uncertainty before fitting

For each output quantity, use at least three timestep levels at fixed fine mesh,
three mesh levels at fixed sufficiently small timestep, and tighter nonlinear /
contact tolerances. Keep geometry and constitutive parameters fixed. Check
contact discretization independently of bulk deformation resolution. Preserve
initial mass, COM, inertia and elastic state when comparing representations.
Convergence means changes fall below a predeclared output budget; the last two
levels alone are not a proof. Monitor contact onset, integrated impulse, outgoing
velocity/spin, maximal deformation, elastic vibration and energy accounting.
Store the reference uncertainty with its results. Do not fit below that noise.

Run for the same physical duration in all methods. Include short collision
windows for detailed comparisons AND long sequences to measure accumulated
bias. Long duration is not itself increased numerical accuracy. Stiff explicit
methods require stable timesteps; implicit methods still need temporal
convergence and a check for artificial damping.

3. Material characterization, then effective-model calibration

Establish density, elastic and rate-dependent material response from published
measurements or dedicated material tests. Preserve these physical parameters
across fidelity levels. Derive coarse spring/beam stiffness from geometry and
material response, then calibrate any reduced-model corrections jointly over
multiple cases. Separate these corrections from measured material parameters.
Fit identifiable contact parameters (surface friction, tangential history,
rolling moment law and damping) to relevant force/motion measurements or the
verified detailed model. If only rebound is observed, stiffness and damping
may be nonidentifiable; contact duration and force/deformation histories help.

Treat restitution as a measured/effective collision output if a compliant model
already generates it. Do not independently impose and fit rebound plus damping
in ways that count the same dissipation twice. Effective closures can depend on
cell size, contact size and speed, but that dependency must be explicit, bounded
and tested. Use shared parameters for a material/surface pair, not a new free
parameter vector for every shape. Distinguish constitutive rate dependence from
numerical damping introduced by the fast integrator.

Use bounded joint optimization with losses normalized separately by physically
meaningful velocity, spin, force, impulse and deformation scales. Include
parameter uncertainty, passivity and conservation checks. A low fitting error
cannot compensate for penetration, missing contacts or incompatible material
behavior. Preserve elastic/contact history during fidelity changes.

4. Hold out complete cases

Split by geometry and impact conditions BEFORE fitting. Keep all nearly
identical variants of a case in one split. Fit on the calibration cases, choose
model complexity/switch thresholds on a separate validation set, then freeze
parameters and test on unseen shapes, speeds, incidence angles, initial spin,
mass/stiffness contrast and numbers of simultaneous contacts. Reserve an
untouched experimental set to assess physical accuracy where data exist.

The catalog roles are initial suggestions. Different published IPC scenes use
different constitutive laws and numerical material values; do not assume one
unconditioned parameter vector describes all of them. Use material identity as
an input, or construct clearly labeled matched-material variants. Original
public cases remain available for direct reproduction.

5. Compare what is physically meaningful

For isolated impacts: outgoing linear/angular velocity, total impulse and
moment impulse, contact duration, peak force, maximal deformation, residual
vibration and plastic change if modeled. Force-peak comparisons need temporal
resolution; impulse alone does not establish force accuracy.

For long runs: mechanical energy plus dissipated energy minus external work,
linear/angular momentum balance including external reactions, resting support,
settling time, contact loads, penetration and failure rates. In chaotic piles,
compare ensemble distributions and aggregate observables after divergence,
rather than demand matching every late trajectory. Record repeated runs/seeds.

Do not mix velocity and spin into an arbitrary-length norm for scoring. Declare
separate unitful tolerances or a physically justified kinetic-energy metric.
Do not permit time shifting of curves in a way that hides incorrect impact time.
Near-zero reference quantities require an absolute-error floor.

6. Teach and evaluate the adaptive selector

Run every scene with fast-only, intermediate-only, detailed-only and adaptive
settings. Measure error and cost as a function of cell resolution, timestep,
contact resolution and solver tolerance. Choose the lowest measured cost level
that satisfies each required output tolerance. Use validation data to calibrate
the selector from cheap online indicators: contact residual, estimated strain,
contact duration versus wave transit time, friction transition margin, material
contrast and coupled contact-island structure.

Report false-safe decisions (the selector chose cheap but missed its tolerance),
missed-contact/instability cases, escalation rate and time spent at each level.
Use conservative fallback outside the calibrated regime. Hysteresis and a
minimum dwell time reduce oscillatory switching, while severe new contacts may
still require immediate escalation. A data-fit error predictor is not a proof
of an error bound; establish held-out coverage and check selected steps with a
more detailed solve where practical.

Include explicit switch tests: loaded spring/beam before impact; spinning body
with sticking history; changing contact normal; dense contact island; strong
material interface. Track state-transfer cost and any changes in momentum,
stored energy and dissipated energy. Refinement/reduction must not silently
discard stored energy or invent unobserved fine-scale history.

7. Fair performance reporting

Compare methods at matched observable accuracy, with the same physical horizon,
collision geometry, hardware, compiler/build mode and output frequency. Count
collision detection, adaptation, state transfer and solver work. Separate
startup/JIT from steady state, repeat timings, and report median and tail latency
as well as memory. Show error-versus-wall-time curves rather than one FPS number.
An optimized continuum or reduced model is a stronger comparison than an
intentionally overresolved or unoptimized reference.

Immediate implementation dependency

The repository currently provides a circle prototype and local frozen-contact
research code, not an arbitrary-shape production engine or continuum backend.
Use this repository for the engine; define its adapter API, dimensionality and reference backend
before implementing adapters. For 2D, specify plane stress / plane strain and
thickness; 3D twisting and plate transverse bending cannot be substituted for
planar collision tests. The downloaded inputs are benchmark assets, not a claim
that an engine comparison or material validation has already succeeded.
