# Independent integration review

The actual native opposing-wall control confirms the clearance defect and its correction at production source `bca35c3103a78c731b37ea8a467e5fc71d13e8aa`. With radius 0.1 m, mass 1 kg, inertia 0.004 kg m², initial speed 0.95 m/s, zero gravity/friction/restitution and a 1 ms step, the old gap policy ends at x = 1.050 mm and penetrates the right wall by 49.999 µm. The combined policy ends at x = 0.950 mm with 50.001 µm clearance and zero numerical pose displacement. Both retain speed 0.95 m/s and kinetic energy 0.45125 J; native physical and position residuals are zero. The intentionally overlapping initial state isolates numerical position integration.

The integration sequence is correct in this review: the original full physical Coulomb solve accepts first; a copied solver-body pool then receives every accepted-minus-warm impulse in upstream row/A/B order; its signed normal rate includes linear and angular motion, external force and gyro. The position target subtracts this accepted physical rate from the desired combined rate. Only normal split impulses are written, their mobility uses translation alone, and fake angular turn is cleared after upstream impulse writeback. Position solve counters and maximum residual update only after acceptance. Saved final and principal-frame progress ledgers agree exactly. `source-review.json` records source lines and hashes.

The production helper differs from the independently compiled upstream writeback oracle at published `f66193fc8cfa0329cbdad0eb2854ecaedce8ab4a` only in its first comment. That earlier actual C++ oracle exercised warm deltas, two tangents, rotating kinematic and fixed slots, full angular rates, external force/gyro and anisotropic inertia. This collinear native-world control has no torque and cannot independently establish those noncollinear cases.

Two harness failures are retained. The v1 fixture put density on the body, which the adapter ignores; actual mass was 0.0041887902 kg. The v2 fixture corrected density to the sphere shape, then its assertion incorrectly required an optional early-recovery diagnostic that native output omits when that policy is disabled. No physical assertion failed in v2. The prospective v3 plan reused the exact hashed v2 old-policy raw result and progress, then executed the previously unexecuted combined policy. The old results, plans, launchers and both failure records remain unchanged. No production, test or build file was written; no compiler ran. Early recovery was false in both controls, so this comparison isolates the position policy without invoking that schedule.

This is a native 3D sphere/plane control, not a nonlinear contact-geometry proof, a friction or calibrated rubber test, or an accuracy order. Combined targets remain a first-order frozen-Jacobian construction; rotation, geometry changes and unavailable translation-only corrections remain limitations. The separately completed six-run study retained five complete physical-eligible histories and one strict velocity rejection, with zero qualified references: the rejected middle level blocks both original seed42 refinement edges, and both seed7301 edges fail by large margins. No successful prefix, skip over a rejected level or changed energy/error gate establishes accuracy.
