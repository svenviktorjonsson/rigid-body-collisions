\noindent This note supports an executable native 3D benchmark. It does not
introduce a novel friction law, claim measured material accuracy, or qualify the
failed frictional trajectory references. The implemented optimized profile is
frictionless and inelastic. Friction and restitution remain available in the
separate pinned Bullet baseline.

\section{Three-dimensional bodies and point impulses}
Positive action goes from B to A. Lever arms are relative to their respective
centres of mass. Use the wedge matrix
\begin{equation}
 r^{\wedge}=\begin{pmatrix}0&-r_z&r_y\\r_z&0&-r_x\\-r_y&r_x&0\end{pmatrix},
 \quad r^{\wedge}p=r\times p,
 \quad v_c=v+(r^{\wedge})^{\mathsf T}\omega.
\end{equation}
With normal impulse magnitude $p_k\geq0$ and normal $\hat n_k$ from B to A,
\begin{align}
 \Delta p_A&=\hat n_k p_k,&\Delta L_A&=r_A^{\wedge}\hat n_k p_k,\\
 \Delta p_B&=-\hat n_k p_k,&\Delta L_B&=-r_B^{\wedge}\hat n_k p_k.
\end{align}
The world inertia is the full tensor
$\mathbb I_i=R_i\mathbb I_{i,\mathrm{body}}R_i^{\mathsf T}$, not a scalar
or a world-fixed diagonal. Authored geometry determines mass, centre and tensor;
compound fixtures include all parallel-axis terms. A principal-frame transform
supplies Bullet with diagonal body inertia without discarding off-diagonal terms.

\section{A many-body contact graph}
For a temporary reference length $\ell>0$, stack each dynamic body's
$V_i=(v_i,\ell\omega_i)^{\mathsf T}$ and use
\begin{equation}
 H_i=\begin{pmatrix}m_i&0\\0&\mathbb I_i/\ell^2\end{pmatrix},\qquad
 \Delta P_i=\begin{pmatrix}\Delta p_i\\\Delta L_i/\ell\end{pmatrix}.
\end{equation}
Scalar square blocks implicitly multiply the appropriate identity. The length
only makes the mixed velocity and work pairing convenient; consistent scaling
leaves physical results unchanged. Stack dynamic $V_i$ and block-diagonal $H_i$.
The normal contact row for body A is
\begin{equation}
 G_{k,A}=\begin{pmatrix}\hat n_k^{\mathsf T}&
 \hat n_k^{\mathsf T}(r_A^{\wedge})^{\mathsf T}/\ell\end{pmatrix};
\end{equation}
body B has the negative counterpart. Bodies not participating in a contact have
zero blocks; each contact point is a separate row. Prescribed boundaries have
zero inverse mobility and enter the relative normal velocity explicitly:
\begin{align}
 u^-&=GV^-+u_{\rm prescribed},&K&=GH^{-1}G^{\mathsf T},\\
 \Delta V&=H^{-1}G^{\mathsf T}p,&u^+&=u^-+Kp.
\end{align}
For a prescribed B, its contribution is
$-\hat n_k^{\mathsf T}\bigl(v_B+(r_B^{\wedge})^{\mathsf T}\omega_B\bigr)$.
This is an external actuator; its work must not be omitted.

\section{Inelastic unilateral contact and energy}
The zero-restitution normal solve is the convex quadratic program
\begin{equation}
 \min_{p\geq0}\;\tfrac12p^{\mathsf T}Kp+(u^-)^{\mathsf T}p,
 \qquad p\geq0,\quad u^+\geq0,\quad p_k u_k^+=0.
\end{equation}
All incident impulses act through the same matrix, so propagation is simultaneous.
With wall work $W_{\rm boundary}=-p^{\mathsf T}u_{\rm prescribed}$,
\begin{align}
 \Delta T-W_{\rm boundary}
 &=p^{\mathsf T}u^-+\tfrac12p^{\mathsf T}Kp\\
 &=-\tfrac12p^{\mathsf T}Kp\leq0.
\end{align}
This frozen-geometry statement is separate from finite-step integration,
gravity and position stabilization. Pressure can be nonunique for redundant
normals, while the velocity change is unique. The implementation uses Cholesky
on positive definite active faces, an active set when bounds bind, and explicit
feasibility/complementarity checks. Singular or failed faces disclose fallback;
no diagonal compliance or regularization is added.

\section{An exact reduction, not ignored friction}
The Bullet baseline has one normal and two tangent rows per contact. With zero
friction, both tangent impulses are fixed to zero. Partitioning its mobility,
\begin{equation}
 \begin{pmatrix}K_{nn}&K_{nt}\\K_{tn}&K_{tt}\end{pmatrix}
 \begin{pmatrix}p_n\\0\end{pmatrix}
 =\begin{pmatrix}K_{nn}p_n\\K_{tn}p_n\end{pmatrix}.
\end{equation}
Only the normal unknowns and normal inequalities remain; tangent velocity is
allowed to slip. Eliminating fixed-zero rows \emph{before} assembly gives a
$c\times c$ scalar mobility instead of $3c\times3c$: nine times less matrix
payload. Eliminating after assembly gives the same physical and algebraic
solution but retains unnecessary construction and storage work. The benchmark
holds the algorithm, geometry, materials, step guard and integration convention
fixed and verifies bitwise-identical state histories. The reduction cannot be
applied to nonzero friction, where normal--tangent coupling must remain.

\section{The 100 m/s wall test and its scope}
An exactly touching row, or packed 3D grid of unit-mass spheres, starts at rest
in zero gravity. A prescribed wall/container translates at $U$, with no friction,
restitution or position projection. The inelastic simultaneous solution is
\begin{equation}
 v_i^+=U,\quad\omega_i^+=0,\quad x_i(t)=x_i(0)+Ut,\quad
 W_{\rm boundary}=N\lVert U\rVert^2,\quad T^+=\tfrac12N\lVert U\rVert^2.
\end{equation}
The fixtures declare initial overlap near $10^{-10}$ m solely to make
floating-point touching-contact discovery stable; it must stay below $10^{-8}$ m.
Contacts use start-of-update wall poses with commanded velocities; walls then
advance independently after dynamic integration. This avoids treating one wall
travel increment as preexisting penetration. No contents are teleported.

Native collision discovery and orientation are genuinely 3D. The benchmark has
64-body rows along all three axes, a 128-body row, and packed 27/64-body boxes.
Every repetition checks all-body trajectories, velocity, spin, penetration,
containment, wall work and fallback counts before reporting timing ratios.
This ideal normal-contact evidence does not certify chaotic frictional paths,
arbitrary initial overlap, all concave swept collisions, experimental materials,
or a convergence order for the full engine. Pinned Bullet mechanics and standard
active-set QP methods are prior art; the measured change is an implementation and
verification result.
