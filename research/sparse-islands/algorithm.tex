\author{Physics-engine implementation note}

\begin{document}\maketitle
\noindent The implementation is planar and acts on an already discovered, frozen
contact graph. The accompanying measured report establishes kernel performance,
not complete-engine throughput or a new theory of collision. Geometry, masses,
inertias and material coefficients remain unchanged when selecting an algorithm.

\section{Body and contact operators}
For each body use $\widetilde U_i=(v_{ix},v_{iy},\ell\omega_i)$, where the
arbitrary reference length $\ell>0$ is a representation choice. Inverse mass is
stored as a vector rather than a dense diagonal matrix:
\begin{equation}
 H_i^{-1}=\operatorname{diag}(m_i^{-1},m_i^{-1},\ell^2/\mathbb I_i).
 \qquad r^{\wedge}=\begin{bmatrix}-r_y&r_x\end{bmatrix}.
\end{equation}
At an edge going from B to A, a direction $d$ contributes the row
\begin{equation}
 G_d=\begin{bmatrix}
 d_x&d_y&(r_A^{\wedge}d)/\ell&
 -d_x&-d_y&-(r_B^{\wedge}d)/\ell
 \end{bmatrix}.
\end{equation}
Use one normal and tangent row per point, not one summed impulse per body pair.
Prescribed degrees of freedom have zero inverse mass and retain their supplied
velocity in the relative-motion map. Sparse operators give
\begin{equation}
 V^-=G\widetilde U^-,\quad K=G H^{-1}G^{\mathsf T},\quad
 V^+=V^-+K\Delta P,\quad
 \widetilde U^+=\widetilde U^-+H^{-1}G^{\mathsf T}\Delta P.
\end{equation}
No inverse of the full, potentially singular contact mobility is required.
World geometry/mass changes invalidate this fixed-geometry cache. Sparse
factorisation may have substantial fill on highly connected graphs; linear row
scaling is not a theorem about all contact graphs.

\section{Normal active-set solve}
For zero restitution and frictionless normals, solve the standard convex QP
\begin{equation}
 \min_{p_n\geq0}\;\tfrac12p_n^{\mathsf T}K_{nn}p_n
       +(V_n^-)^{\mathsf T}p_n.
 \qquad V_n^+\geq0,\quad p_n\odot V_n^+=0.
\end{equation}
Free active rows are solved together; a candidate crossing a bound moves only
to its first bound. Wrong bound gradients release the corresponding variables.
Singular free blocks use least squares without introducing compliance. Cycling,
inconsistent stationarity or excessive work rejects the candidate. This block
scheme has no claimed universal convergence guarantee.

The acceptance gate checks nonnegative impulses, normal feasibility and the
velocity at contacts carrying positive impulse. Raw complementarity products
are also reported; their scale grows with impulse magnitude. The benchmark
requires every ball velocity within $10^{-8}$ m/s and wall reaction within
$10^{-5}$ kg m/s. Small islands may choose dense factorisation of the same QP;
the contact-count threshold is a numerical policy, not a physical parameter.

\section{Implicit Coulomb residual and exact structural dispatch}
One nonnegative coefficient $\mu$ defines the implemented inelastic law:
\begin{align}
 &p_n\geq0,\quad V_n^+\geq0,\quad p_n V_n^+=0,\\
 &|p_t|\leq\mu p_n,\qquad
 V_t^+\ne0\ \Longrightarrow\ p_t=-\mu p_n\operatorname{sign}(V_t^+).
\end{align}
At $V_t^+=0$, friction may lie inside its capacity. Final sticking does not
constitute elastic tangential rebound. No separate static/dynamic coefficients,
elastic-history state, positive restitution or free rolling couple is provided.

Positive diagonal numerical scales $\rho_n,\rho_t$ give an equivalent root system:
\begin{align}
 F_n(p)&=p_n-\max(0,p_n-\rho_n V_n^+),\\
 F_t(p)&=p_t-\operatorname{clip}(p_t-\rho_t V_t^+,
                  -\mu\max(p_n,0),\mu\max(p_n,0)).
\end{align}
The implementation uses a sparse generalised Jacobian and a merit line search.
A disclosed sparse least-squares fallback is allowed; optimiser success is
never a replacement for mechanical residual checks. Four held-out stress cases
remain rejected. No existence or uniqueness conclusion follows from rejection.

If and only if the assembled cross-block has exact structural zero,
$K_{nt}=0$, the normal pressure can be solved first and tangential response is
the bounded QP
\begin{equation}
 \min_{-\mu p_n\leq p_t\leq\mu p_n}
 \tfrac12p_t^{\mathsf T}K_{tt}p_t+(V_t^-)^{\mathsf T}p_t.
\end{equation}
Its KKT conditions give sticking or saturated friction opposite final slip.
This is the same implicit law in this decoupled case, not a conic-QP replacement
for general hard Coulomb contact. Small nonzero cross terms are not dropped.
Nonunique normal pressures can produce different friction outcomes; the selected
active-set pressure distribution is not an authenticated material force history.

\section{Mechanical passivity with driven walls}
Separate prescribed relative motion as $V_{\rm prescribed}$. The instantaneous
external work and change in dynamic-body kinetic energy obey
\begin{align}
 W_{\rm boundary}&=-V_{\rm prescribed}^{\mathsf T}\Delta P,\\
 \Delta T-W_{\rm boundary}
 &= (V^-)^{\mathsf T}\Delta P+	frac12\Delta P^{\mathsf T}K\Delta P\\
 &= (V^+)^{\mathsf T}\Delta P-	frac12\Delta P^{\mathsf T}K\Delta P\leq0.
\end{align}
The last inequality follows from normal complementarity, dissipative Coulomb
slip and $K\succeq0$. The implementation also checks it numerically. Content
kinetic energy may increase when the actuator supplies work.

For the closed unit-mass row driven at $1\,\mathrm{m/s}$,
$1\leq v_{1x}^+\leq\cdots\leq v_{Nx}^+\leq1$ forces every ball to $1\,\mathrm{m/s}$.
Thus momentum is $N$, kinetic energy $N/2$, wall work $N$ and dissipation $N/2$
in the corresponding SI units. The independent audit uses explicit row force,
torque and point-velocity formulas rather than the new sparse assembler.

\section{Scope of the result}
The performance report retains every timing sample, 139 snapshots, source archives
and rejected inputs. Long-row gains justify investigating a sparse island kernel
in the actual engine. They do not establish full moving-container convergence,
accurate stress/elastic waves, continuous collision discovery or a universal
solver. The coefficients are synthetic. The compiler handover retains language
unit/shape semantics and independent native/WASM/hardware acceptance boundaries.
